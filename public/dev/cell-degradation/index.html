Assumptions and what this demo leaves out
  • Original site: 400 MWh LV reference energy. Added units are installed at year 0 and age alongside the original fleet.
  • Both cohorts lose 2.5% capacity annually. SOC imbalance makes 8% of their remaining reference energy inaccessible.
  • Annual average offline capacity is fixed at 8% of remaining energy for both cohorts, separate from SOC imbalance.
  • Conversion consumes 6% of accessible energy. Aging, imbalance, offline, and conversion losses scale with the combined fleet.
  • Original plan means no augmentation at the selected age. The floating delta spans original-plan capacity to selected-site capacity.
  • This is a full-charge energy screen, not instantaneous SOC. Power, thermal limits, and actual site performance are not evaluated.

Assumptions and what this demo leaves out
  • Every curve uses invented prices and a fictional site. ERCOT-style describes the example, not historical ERCOT data.
  • Each day is optimized with perfect price foresight, 15-minute intervals, and no simultaneous battery charging and discharging.
  • Usable capacity is AC-discharge-equivalent MWh. The base battery can deliver 100 MW for two hours from full.
  • Round-trip efficiency is 90%. Storage starts and ends empty; no initial energy is supplied for free.
  • Battery charging and discharging each have a 100 MW limit. Combined site export never exceeds 100 MW.
  • Solar is capped at 150 MW generation. Solar-only charging and curtailment are allowed; grid charging is disabled.
  • Other scenarios allow up to 100 MW grid import. Peak support earns the displayed fictional delivery credit.
  • Operating margin equals export receipts minus grid purchases and battery variable cost, plus any peak-delivery credit.
  • Battery variable cost is $3 per MWh discharged. Negative-price grid purchases appear as a credit, not a positive cost.
  • Added capacity costs $140/kWh installed and $4/kWh-year maintenance. Capital recovery assumes 8% over 15 years.
  • Daily capacity cost allocates annualized capital and maintenance over 365 days; it does not annualize this day’s earnings.
  • Existing fixed costs cancel in incremental comparisons. Taxes, replacement, terminal costs, market qualification, and actual investment returns are excluded.

Animation assumptions and financial conventions
  • Source: V4 animation’s site_plan_model.json and finance.py. This remains an illustrative model, not site data.
  • Original nameplate is 100,000 kWh across three legacy blocks. Initial installed cost is $300/kWh, or $30 million.
  • Legacy capacity declines 2% annually; added cohorts decline 1%. Revenue follows usable capacity, not installed nameplate.
  • Gen 2 costs $210/kWh; Gen 3 costs $147/kWh in today’s dollars, before the selected sensitivity multiplier.
  • Installed costs, revenue, and $8/kWh-year O&M inflate 2.5% annually. O&M applies to installed augmentation capacity.
  • The animation books installation spending and a full year’s added operating income in the same integer year.
  • Year 15 credits unused block life using inflated generation cost and straight-line remaining life over 15 years.
  • Residual value is a terminal valuation assumption, not contracted sale proceeds. The breakdown also shows NPV without this credit.
  • Added capital compares discounted augmentation spend with original installed cost. Added profit includes residual value, matching the animation.
  • No dispatch constraints, charging-cost settlement, outage costs, taxes, or debt are added to this source scenario.

Assumptions and what this demo leaves out
  • Independent triangular cost and revenue multipliers span the selected ± range, centered on your finance inputs.

  • Delay shifts all four additions. Campaigns after year 15 are excluded; no purchase commitments are assumed.
  • Draw shares and percentiles describe invented scenarios. They are not calibrated probabilities or investment recommendations.
  • The animation’s residual-value convention is retained. Correlated risks, outages, market qualification, and real price forecasts are not sampled.
