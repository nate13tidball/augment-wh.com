Augment | Technical solution

    • Every battery fades, about 2% of capacity each year.
    • A site that starts at 100% is near 74% by year 15.
    • Lost capacity is lost revenue, so plan for it from day one.
    • A typical site: grid, site meter, step-down transformer and three Company A blocks.
    • A legacy battery controller runs the original blocks.
    • Year 0: 100% of original capacity, with open space on site.
    • By year 4 each block has faded to about 92%.
    • Nothing is broken. This is normal degradation.
    • The site already delivers less than it was built for.
    • By year 8 the site is near 85%.
    • The legacy controller only manages the blocks it shipped with.
    • Adding another supplier's batteries is not simple.
    • By year 12 the site is near 78%.
    • Revenue keeps falling with capacity.
    • The original plan ends near 74% at year 15.
    • A quarter of the capacity, and its revenue, is gone.
    • The interconnection and open space sit underused.
    • Back to year 4, now with the augmented site plan.
    • The Universal Battery Controller reads the site meter.
    • It commands the legacy controller instead of replacing it.
    • It runs ANY block from ANY vendor.
    • Augmentation goes in one block at a time, only when needed.
    • Year 4: a Company B block at $210/kWh installed.
    • Year 10: a Company C block at $147/kWh installed.
    • Each round buys newer, lower-cost storage from the best vendor.
    • With the first new block online, the site reaches 113%.
    • The original plan is at 92% in the same year.
    • The profit indicator turns from red to green.
    • Year 7: a second Company B block holds the site near 115%.
    • Augmentation makes up for the legacy blocks' fade.
    • Year 10: Company C, a different vendor, on the same controller.
    • The site is near 118% of original capacity.
    • Year 15: 114% augmented, versus 74% on the original plan.
    • About 40 points of extra capacity on the same interconnection.
    • Open pad space remains for future blocks.
    • The same plan keeps the site above 100% for decades.
    • The original plan fades to about a third by year 50.
    • In year 15, the extra capacity means roughly 20–40% more profit.
    • Base case: about 20% added capital, in present-value terms.
    • It returns about 33% more operating profit.
    • Base IRR is about 14% against an 8% discount rate. High case: about 22%.
    • Model estimates for discussion, not a site-specific proposal.

Illustrative concept and model scenarios only. Costs, performance, and financial results depend on each site’s design and assumptions.
